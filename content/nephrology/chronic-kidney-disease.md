## Why it matters
- CKD affects 47 million people (~15% population) in the US [@goodbred.langan_2023]
- 9/10 do not know they have impared renal function
- More advanced disease is associated w/ increased risk of CVD mortality

## Definition
- CKD = [eGFR < 60mL or albuminuria (>30mg/day) or Alb/Cr >30mg/g or abnormal UA or polycystic/dysplatic kidneys] that lasts for >3 mo

## Risk Stratification
- Kidney Diseaes: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) stages based on eGFR + persistent albuminura b/c proteinuria is associated w/ disease progression regarless of eGFR
- Most common causes: DM, HTN, Nephrotoxic meds, malignancy; anatomic/autoimmne/genetic/infectious/metabolic/obstructive/vascular processes

## Laboratory tests
- Calculate eGFR using CKD-EPI w/o race. 
- Use cystatin C to confirm eGFR b/c is produced at a constant rate, independent of race
- Quantify albuminuria using Ur albumin/Cr ratio from first-morning sample or 24-hr collection to improve accuracy. If ⬆ for 3+ months = CKD. Transient rises possible for more acute processes

## Screening/Indications
- Most pt are asymptomatic
- KDIGO recommends screenign pts w/ CVD, DM, or HTN, pt >60 yrs, FHx CKD, Hx AKI, Hx preeclampsia w/ eGFR and Ur Alb/Cr
- ACP recommonds NOT screening asymptomatic adults w/o risk factors[@lambert_2014]

Note: No RCTs have shown improved outcomes w/ screening asymptomatic people


## Evaluation
- Take history, exam, BP, weight

### Initial Labs
- Urine Albumin/Cr : assess albuminuria
- A1c : assess DM status
- Lipids : Screen for coexisting dyslipidemia
- Renal US : structural abnormalities
- BMP : assess for electrolyte abnormalities
- UA/microscopy : assess for underlying causes of CKD (casts, hematuria, pyuria, tubular issues)
