## Why it matters
- Osteoporosis = a chronic, progressive disease characterized by low bone mass, microarchitecture deterioration of bone tissue, bone fragility, and a consequent increase in fracture risk [@nationalosteoporosisfoundation_2014].
- Roughly 50% of white women and 20% of white men have a fracture related to osteoporosis in their lifetime
- Osteoporosis contributes to Disability, institutionalization in long-term nursing care, mortality, and increased annualized health care costs[@nationalosteoporosisfoundation_2014; @usdepartmentofhealthandhumanservices_2004;@nationalosteoporosisfoundation_2002]
  - 10 million Americans ≥50 yrs have osteoporosis of the hip  
  - 1.5 million Americans have osteoporotic fracture (40% of women and 10% of men will have a fracture of the hip, spine, or wrist) 
  - ==Just 40% regain prefracture independence==

## Screening
- Guidelines vary
  - USPSTF: DEXA for All women ≥ 65, or if 10 yr fracture risk  is ≥ that of 65 yr old woman. Do not screen men
  - National Osteoporosis Foundation: Above + screen men ≥ 70 yrs
- When to repeat: ?? but no more frequent than every 2 years [@americanacademyoffamilyphysicians_2023]
  - WHY? Average BMD loss is estimated to be 1% per year BUT all DEXA scanners have known measurement error ~ 3-5%

## Diagnosis
- Radiographically using Bone Mineral Density (BMD) from central dual energy x-ray absroptiometry (DEXA) w/ T score <-2.5 -OR-
- Presence of a fragility fracture -OR-
- Presence of a vertebral fracture -OR-
- [Osteopenia](osteopenia.md) (DEXA Tscore -1>x>-2.5) + 10 year [WHO FRAX](https://www.fraxplus.org/calculation-tool/$0)* of 20% for major fracutres +/- ≥3% for hip fracture

Note: *Not validated for men <50 yrs, children, or premenopausal women. Then use Z-scores for age and sex norms. Z score <-2.0 is considered abnormal.

## Evaluate for 2ª Osteoporosis
- ~30-50% are expected to be from 2ª cause
- Many medical conditions
  - CNS disturbance
  - COPD
  - Endocrine/Metabolic Disorders
  - GI Disorders, severe Liver disease
  - Hematologic Disorders, HIV
  - Nutritional disorders
  - Kidney disease
  - Rheumatoid arthritis, 
- Medications
  - Anticonvulsants
  - Chemotherapy
  - Depo-Provera
  - Glucocorticoids

  - Heparin

## Consider Evaluation for Vertebral Fractures [@harris.etal_2023]
- AACE/ACE Recommendations
  - Women >70 yrs, men >80 if Tscore <-1
  - Women 65-69, Men 70-79 if Tscore <-1.5
  - People w/ risk factors:
    - Low trauma fracture after age 50
    - Heigh loss of 1.5 in from peak
-  Assess using dedicated spine radiography or request vertebral assessment w/ DEXA
## Treatment
- National Osteoporosis Foundation = Treat if:
  - Hx fragility/vertebral fracture
  - FRAX Tscore <-2.5
  - FRAX Tscore -1>x>-2.5 + 10yr risk ≥ 3%
- WHO = Treat if at risk of osteoporosis

### Nonpharmacologic Treatment
- USPSTF: Exercise (weight bearing + balance) + Vit D supplementation + evidence based fall prevention interventions
- Quit smoking 🫡
- Discourage heavy alcohol consumption (4+ for men, 2+ for women)
- Decrease Caffeine (<2.5 cups coffee/day)
- Nutritious diet + Vitamin D (800 IU/day) and Calcium (1,200 IUD/day)Supplementation 

### Pharmacologic Treatment
#### Bisphosphonates
- 1st line!
- How they work: Inhibit osteoclastic activity and prevent resorption
- Oral: Alendronate (Fosamax) and Risendronate (Actonel)
  - Can dose daily, weekly, or monthly
  - Side Effects: GI upset - Take only w/water, wait 30mins before reclining or eating food
- IV: Zoledronic acid (Reclast, annual) and Ibandronate (q3mo)
- Length of therapy: ?? Expert consensus says ~5 years then reassess
- Major adverse effects: Osteonecrosis of jaw (very rare, 1/100,000)

#### Selective estrogen receptor modulators
- *Raloxifene*: Effective for reducing vertebral fracture only. *Side effects*: Increased vasomotor symptoms, Increased risk of thromboembolism, decreased risk of invasive breast cancer
- *Bazedoxifene*: combo therapy w/ conjugated estrogen under name Duavee.

#### Calcitonin (nasal spray)
- How it works: Antiresorptive agent
- Only to prevent vertebral compression fractures.
- Not super effective

#### Teriparatide
- How it works: recombinant human PTH, bone anabolic
- Only for very high risk patients or if bisphosphonates haven't worked
#### Denosumab
- How it works: Human monocolonal-ab that inhibits osteoclasts by blocking nf-kB ligands
- Good news: It works!
- Bad news: You may not be able to stop taking it due to risk of resorption and increased risk of fracture following discontinuation

#### Hormone Therapy
- Questionable efficacy. Low doses of conjugated estrogens + estradiol ⬆BMD but do not improve fracture risk
