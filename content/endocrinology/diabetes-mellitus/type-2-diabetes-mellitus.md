## Overview
## Prevention
### Lifestyle Behavior Change
- Refer all T2DM to diabetes prevention program. Goal: Weight reduction of 5-7% + >150 min/week of physical activity
- Recommend Mediterranean-style, plant-based, DASH, or low-carb diets to all T2DM
### Pharmacologic Interventions
- Metformin
	- *Most useful ppl <59 yrs; Hx of [GDM](gestational-diabetes.md); BMI >= 35; A1c >6; Fasting glucose >110.* 
	- Very useful for people on long term glucocorticoids
	- Check B12 levels annually as metformin can cause low levels
	- When paired w/ lifestyle modification = 50% reduction in DM risk
- SGLTi and GLPs +/- GIP
  - Metabolic benefits + weight loss 

## Comprehensive Medical Evaluation and Assessment of Comorbidities
### Bone Health
- DEXA for all >= 65 and >=50 if use insulin or DM for 10+ yrs. Repeat q3yr.
- FRAX is NOT useful in this population as signficantly underestimates
- General Recommendations
  - Ensure adequate intake of protein
  - Ca2+ (1000-1200 mg)
  - D (serum levels 20-30 mg/mL; greater if taking bisphosphonates)
  - Stop smoking
  - Regular physical activity
- Pharmacological treatment: Consider if *T-score <-2, FRAX >= 3% for hip or 20% for major fracture*
	-  **Denosumab** - shown in phase 3 FREEDOM study to be efficacious. MUST be able to maintain the every 6 mo injection or may experience rebound bone loss. 
	- **Bisphosophonates** - may be more appropriate if gaps are expected 
	- **Teriperatide** - Osteoanabolic. Only for very high risk
### Cancer
- Increased risk of cancers of liver, pancreas, endometrium, colon, rectum, breast, and bladder
- Undergo age appropriate screenings
### Cognitive Impairment/Dementia
- Significantly increased risk and rate of cognitive decline
### Dental Care
- Periodontal disease is more severe w/ higher A1c 
- Discuss oral health with all diabetics
- May need to adjust therapy during procedures
### Disability
- Assess for disability and functional decline annually (ADLs and IADLs)
- Assess for  [Frailty](../../geriatric-medicine/frailty.md) annually
- Behavioral Health -  Anxiety, Depression, [Diabetes Distress Scale](https://www.mdcalc.com/calc/10523/diabetes-distress-scale-dds17$0) (DDS) 
### Sexual Health in Men
- Screen for erectile dysfunction (52.5%). Treat w/ PDE5i
- Hypogonadism is common
### Sexual Health in Women
-  Female sexual dysfunction is very common - consider screening with the [Female Sexual Function Index (FSFI)](https://www.apta.org/contentassets/a10dbafd45234c7a98ae3dd98385cdab/fsfi_test.pdf$0). Score ≤ 26 is (+) 
- Treatment -> Lifestyle factors, improve glycemic management, hormonal therapies, consider FDA-approved meds for hypoactive sexual desire disorder
### Metabolic Dysfunction-Associated Steatotic Liver Disease ([MASLD](../../gastroenterology/liver/metabolic-associated-steatotic-liver-disease.md)) and [Metabolic Dysfunction-Associated Steatohepatitis](../../gastroenterology/liver/metabolic-dysfunction-associated-steatohepatitis.md) (MASH)
- *Screen all adults with T2DM for MASH spectrum*  using FIB-4 score even if they have normal liver enzymes
  - FIB-4: estimates risk of hepatic cirrhosis. Age, AST, ALT, platelets. 
    - <1.3 is low risk
    - ≥1.3 is higher risk of clinically significant fibrosis;  Get a Liver Stiffness Measurement. <8.0kPa has good NPV. Continue to follow in Primary Care w/ repeat testing q3yrs. 
    - ≥2.67 high risk of advanced fibrosis ->  refer to GI w/o additional testing
- “The goal of screening for MASLD is to identify people with at-risk MASH to prevent future cirrhosis, HCC, liver transplantation, and all-cause mortality (193–196).” pg S76
- ~70% of people w/T2DM have MASLD
- ~50% of T2DM + MASLD have MASH
- Management
  - Weight loss: 5-10% loss improves liver histology. Metabolic surgery can improve MASH and cardiometabolic health.
  - Semaglutide is FDA approved for treatment of MASH.
  - Pioglitazone has also been shown to reverse steatohepatitis. 
  - Use statins unless decompensated cirrhosis. 
### Obstructive Sleep Apnea
- Refer for sleep study if indicated

## Glycemic Goals, Hypoglycemia, and Hyperglycemic Crises
### Glycemic Goals
- A1c <7.0%
- Preprandial plasma glucose 80-130 mg/dL
- Peak postprandial plasma glucose <180mg/dL
### Hypoglycemia assessment, prevention, and treatment
- Definitions:
  - Level 1: 54<= Glucose <70 mg/dL
  - Level 2: Glucose <54
  - Level 3: Severe event requiring assistance
- **Risk Assessment**: Prior Level 2/3 events = strongest risk factors. Meds that cause hypoglycemia: *Insulin, Sulfonylureas, Meglitinides*. Screen for hypoglycemia unawareness (hypoglycemia-associated autonomic failure) annually. "Can you always feel when your BG is low?"
- **Treatment**: 15g fast-acting carbs @ alert value of 70 mg/dL, recheck glucose after 15 mins. Glucagon if needed
- **Prevention**
  - Diabetes Education
  - CGMs
  - Modification of DM treatment during illness
    - Hold Metformin and SGLT2i if not PO or AKI occurs
    - Hold GLP-1s in GI illness
    - Hold sulfonylureas w/ Abx and azoles
  - If hypoglycemic unaware, relax glycemic goals for a few weeks to allow counterregulation to reoccur and support hypoglycemic awareness
### Hyperglycemic Crises (DKA, HHS)
- Risk factors include very-low carb diets, fasting, dehydration, alcohol, and autoimmunity 
- Provide w/ ketone tests and educate on ketotic states. Can self manage w/ frequent glucose/ketone monitoring + SQ insulin

## Obesity and Weight Management
- Consistent evidence that obesity management can delay the progression from prediabetes to T2DM and that its beneficial in T2DM. 
- BMI is imperfect but is a fast heuristic and is a close approximation of excess adiposity in most people
- *Check out The Obesity Association's guidelines to recognize and address weight bias and stigma*
### Behavioral Interventions
- The only thing that works are *intensive* lifestyle interventions. 
- No single nutrition/diet plan is best
- Consider use of Weight (-) or (~) rather than (+) insulin, sulfonylureas, meglitinides, or thiazolidinediones
### Metabolic Surgery
- Rapid and strong reduction of risk across multiple domains
- Types: Vertical sleeve gastrectomy,  Roux-en-Y gastric bypass - Both result in a smaller stomach pouch and changes in enteroendocrine hormones. 
- Can use GLP +/- GIP for additional weight loss
### Obesity Pharmacotherapy
- GLP-1 and GIP/GLP-1 classes are best for weight and glucose management. They are some of the only meds that reduce CVD events!
- Weight loss is typically less than what is seen in ppl w/o DM
- Considerations
	- **Tirzepatide** may reduce the efficacy of OCP due to delayed gastric emptying - must use back up contraception for 4 weeks after all dose increases.
	- Nutritional Deficiencies - Vit D is most common
	- Discontinue Sulfonylureas w/ GLP +/- GIPs
- Monitoring: Montly x3, then q3mo

## Pharmacology for Glycemic Control in T2DM

![Flowchart: choosing glucose-lowering medications in type 2 diabetes. One path for cardiovascular and kidney risk reduction (ASCVD, high CVD risk, heart failure, CKD) using GLP-1 RA and SGLT2i; one for weight and glycemic goals, with medications ranked by efficacy; then agents for MASLD or MASH](images/t2dm-glucose-lowering-medications.png)
*Use of glucose-lowering medications in the management of type 2 diabetes. Source: [@ADA_2026]*

- Most common practice is to start with Metformin (although no CVD/CKD benefit) - pill, few side effects (GI), cheap
- ==All T2DM adults w/ risk of ASCVD, HF +/- CKD therapy should  include SGLT2i or GLP1 regardless of A1c==
- When A1c is >= 1.5% above goal use SGLT2i adn GLP1 therapy.
- CVD, CKD, HF: GLP/GIPs > GLP1s > SGLT2i (canagliflozin and empagliflozin).
- MASLD or MASH: Use Pioglitazone + GLP1 (semaglutide)
- Start **insulin** when BG >=300mg/dL or A1c>10%
	- Start w/ basal insulin (0.1-0.2 u/kg/day)
		- Purpose of Basal = restrain hepatic glucose production to limit hyperglycemia overnight/between meals
		- Need to understand how to convert between two different basals, consider 10-20% dose reduction to prevent night time hypoglycemia
		- Be aware of ==overbasalization== (masks insufficient prandial glucose). Signs  = high daily glucose variability (>50mg/dL), hypoglycemic events. 
  - Basal + Prandial
    - Start when fasting BG achieved but A1c still too high or w/ overbasalization
    - Make sure are on GLP or GIP/GLP first before starting prandial!
    - Start w/ single dose @ largest meal of the day. 4 units or 10% of basal insulin. 
  - T2DMs typically require higher daily doses (~1u/kg) due to resistance and are not sensitive to human regular insulin like T1DMs.
  - Delivery mechanisms: All insulins are available in more concentrated dosing. Pumps, patches, and inhaled options available. 
  - All T2DMs using insulin should be offered CGM and AID if injecting insulin
  - Glucagon: all individuals on insulin should be prescribed premixed glucagon for an emergency.
